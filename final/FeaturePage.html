Ramapo Valley Reservation

Introduction Wildlife Outlooks Scenery Conclusion

The Start of the Hike

Now that the weather has finally started warming up again, my sister and I have been going on a lot more hikes. Most of the time we do not really plan much beforehand. We throw some water bottles into a backpack, check the weather once, and drive until we find somewhere interesting. This time we ended up at Ramapo Valley Reservation, and honestly I was not expecting it to be as nice as it was. The trails, lakes, overlooks, and forests all made the hike feel a lot bigger than I originally thought it would.

Since I spend a lot of time looking for places to stargaze, I am always paying attention to locations that might be worth revisiting at night. Open skies and lower light pollution obviously matter, but safety matters too. Hiking trails feel completely different once it gets dark, especially in wooded areas, so I usually like exploring places during the daytime first. Ramapo immediately felt like one of those locations that would probably look even better at night.

This visual essay follows that first hike through the reservation and the different environments we came across along the way. Some parts of the trail felt quiet and enclosed while others suddenly opened into huge overlooks. The photos throughout the page capture the scenery itself, but also the overall feeling of moving through the reservation over the course of the day.

Wildlife Along the Trail

One of the first things I noticed during the hike was how active everything felt even early in the morning. A lot of the reservation stays heavily wooded, so wildlife shows up pretty often along the trails. It feels different from smaller parks where you barely see anything besides squirrels or birds.

The deer stood there for a few seconds before disappearing back into the trees. Moments like that slow the hike down in a good way because everyone actually stops paying attention to their phones for a minute. The reservation starts feeling less like a regular park and more like actual wilderness once you run into animals this close to the trail.

Near the Water

Once we got closer to the lake, the entire atmosphere changed. Everything felt quieter near the water and the temperature even felt cooler compared to the trails farther uphill. Smaller wildlife became easier to notice too, especially turtles resting near the shoreline and on rocks sticking out of the water.

Compared to the deer earlier in the hike, the lake area felt much calmer and slower. Nothing really felt rushed there. That is one of the things I liked most about the reservation. The scenery keeps changing depending on where the trail leads, so the hike never really starts feeling repetitive.

Looking Over Mahwah

As the trail climbed higher, the forest slowly started opening up into overlooks. These elevated areas are probably some of the most recognizable spots in the reservation because the views stretch out for miles compared to the enclosed trails below.

From this overlook, you can see Mahwah spread out below with the mountains surrounding everything in the distance. After spending so much time walking under tree cover, suddenly seeing miles into the distance completely changes the feeling of the hike. It also makes the reservation feel way larger than it first seemed.

The Distant NYC Skyline

One of the coolest parts of the hike was realizing just how far the view extended on a clear day. Off in the distance, the New York City skyline was visible beyond the hills and suburbs. Seeing the city that far away felt strange considering how quiet the reservation itself was.

The skyline almost looked disconnected from the forest surroundings. Earlier that morning we had been driving through highways and traffic, but from the overlook the city felt far removed from everything around us. That contrast ended up being one of the most memorable parts of the entire hike.

Forest Sections

Not every part of the reservation is made up of giant overlooks or lakes. Some of the best sections were honestly just the quieter forest trails connecting everything together. The paths curve through dense trees where the lighting constantly changes depending on the weather and time of day.

These forest sections made the hike feel connected instead of just a series of random viewpoints. Without them, the overlooks and lakes would probably feel more isolated from each other. Walking through the woods between each major spot made the whole hike feel more gradual and natural.

The Lake From Ground Level

Eventually the trail looped back down toward the water where the lake became the main focus again. From ground level, the shoreline felt calm compared to the elevation changes earlier in the hike. The reflections on the water made this area feel quieter than almost anywhere else in the reservation.

A lot of people stopped here to rest, take photos, or just sit by the water for a while before continuing on. It felt like a natural break in the middle of the hike and balanced out some of the steeper sections from earlier.

The Lake From Above

Later in the hike, the trail climbed again and gave a completely different view of the lake from above. From that elevation, it became easier to understand how large the reservation actually is. The lake stretched through the forest while the trails curved around it and disappeared into the trees.

Seeing the same location from two different elevations completely changed how it felt. From the shoreline, the lake seemed calm and enclosed. From above, it suddenly looked much bigger and more connected to the rest of the reservation. It made the last part of the hike feel like everything was finally coming together.

Conclusion

By the end of the hike, Ramapo Valley Reservation felt less like one single trail and more like several different environments connected together. Forests, lakes, wildlife, and overlooks all appeared within the same hike, but each section created a completely different atmosphere. That variety is what makes the reservation stand out compared to shorter trails nearby.

This visual essay follows the hike in chronological order so the experience feels more natural instead of just showing random photos out of context. Moving from wooded trails to overlooks and eventually back toward the lakes reflects how the scenery gradually changes throughout the reservation.

The hike also reminded me how easy it is to find places that feel isolated even within driving distance of cities and suburbs. Despite being relatively close to New York City, parts of the reservation still felt quiet and disconnected from everything outside the trails. In the future, I would like to revisit Ramapo during different seasons and eventually explore how the reservation changes at night for stargazing.
