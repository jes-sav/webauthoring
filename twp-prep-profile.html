Why I’m Staying Home This Summer

When I got on the no fly list, it felt like the world had come crashing down on me. My entire life revolved around travel, and filling up my carry on with as many souvenirs as possible. Collecting these objects and filling my shelf with them was my only motivation throughout my mundane day to day life.

When I was contacted by a foreign travel company and offered a free trip, I couldn’t help but agree before reading the fine print. The small caveat of the trip was that I had to bring a package they sent me on the plane, and little did I know, it would turn out to be a huge mistake.

Long story short, I’ll be stuck in the United States for a while, and because of the ankle monitor the farthest trip I’ll take is probably to my mailbox. Being surrounded by my travel items has made the transition into a convict difficult, but it inspired me to start this project.

In order to save myself the pain of looking at my old souvenirs, while still preserving their memories, I’ve decided to document all of them digitally. Once time has passed and my emotions have settled, I can explore my old travels again.

Overview

This week had a fun mix of objects that I decided to toss. In previous weeks, I had focused on items from specific trips. Last week I hit a milestone and finally cleared out all the stuff from my Egypt trip, and the week before I was able to get rid of everything from my third trip to Scottsdale, Arizona.

Clearing out entire vacations at once was proving to be pretty difficult for me. Seeing everything together made the change feel more permanent, so for this week I decided to approach things a different way. I wanted to grab various items from different trips, and noticed it was significantly less difficult for me this way. I also started to clear out items that I kept in my carry on.

The first collection of items I tossed were mementos from a specific trip. I tossed a mallet I got from a crab shack in Maryland. It was complimentary and came with any order of crabs. It’s wooden with an engraving, but since crabs aren’t part of my weekly menu, I feel ok with getting rid of it. The next object in this set was a seashell. I’m not sure where it’s from, seeing as I have probably hundreds. The shell itself isn't that interesting, and any color it may have originally had faded into a mix of gray and white. My final object is a 20 euro cent I got while in Canada. It’s a small gold coin, and although it’s definitely pretty, it’s not much use to me in the USA.

The second group of items I got rid of focused around the items I would bring with me on flights. The first was a crossword puzzle book. It’s a relatively smaller pink booklet, and I got it to pass time in terminals and during flights. The reason I’m getting rid of it is because it’s actually entirely complete. I was proud of myself for finishing the book, and could never bring myself to get rid of it. However, it serves no purpose other than periodically boosting my ego, so I’m ready for it to go. Finally, I’m throwing away a picture of my dog. The photo was taken by me a few years ago. I thought he looked cute, and I brought it around with me to always remind myself of my favorite dog in the world. I thought that I could never bring myself to get rid of it, but then I looked by my side and realized he was there physically. I feel like there is no need to keep the picture around when the real thing is much better, so I chose to throw it out.

My last group are items that reminded me of specific memories from trips. Although these items all connect to a story or reminiscence, the objects themselves are truly trash. The most obvious one to me is the cap to a spray deodorant can. It’s small with a blue logo on top, and the bottle it was once connected to is long gone. It’s basically completely useless, and it was definitely the easiest object to let go of. My next item was my old phone that I accidentally ran over. It cracked all along the back, parts of the case flew off, and one of the cameras broke completely. I got a replacement, so there is no need for this one anymore. The last object I got was a bracelet that I was scammed into purchasing. It’s a beaded bracelet featuring red, black, and white beads. A young girl who was probably in elementary school approached me and asked if I wanted to support her business. I agreed, and let her make a bracelet for me. When she was done, she told me it would be $15, and since she had already spent the time making it I decided to cough up the money. I can’t wear or touch the bracelet out of embarrassment for paying that much, but couldn’t bring myself to throw it out either because of what I paid for it.
