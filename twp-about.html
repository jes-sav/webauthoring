About This Project

About the Project

Miles of Mess began as a way to deal with an unexpected change in my life. Traveling had always been one of my biggest interests, and over the years I accumulated a large collection of souvenirs, random objects, and travel mementos. These items were small reminders of places I had visited and experiences I had along the way. However, once traveling was no longer an option for me, the collection started to feel less like a set of memories and more like clutter that constantly reminded me of something I had lost.

The goal of this project is to slowly sort through those items and decide what should stay and what should go. Each week I choose a group of objects connected to different trips and document them before throwing them away, giving them away, or exchanging them. By writing about the items and photographing them first, I can preserve the memories without keeping the physical objects. In a way, the project turns everyday trash into a kind of personal archive.

Artists and photographers have explored similar ideas about waste and everyday objects. For example, artist Sarah Newman often works with found materials and discarded objects to highlight the hidden stories behind ordinary things. Photographer Vik Muniz has also created large-scale artworks using trash and recycled materials, showing how objects that are normally thrown away can still hold artistic and cultural value. Meanwhile, artist Yayoi Kusama is known for exploring repetition and accumulation, themes that relate closely to the way collections can grow over time without us even noticing.

Outside of the art world, conversations about consumption and waste have become increasingly important. Many people are beginning to rethink how much they buy and keep, whether through minimalism, decluttering movements, or sustainability efforts. Projects like minimalism and discussions about waste management encourage people to think more carefully about the objects they accumulate and what eventually happens to them.

Miles of Mess exists somewhere between a personal diary and a small documentation project. It is not just about getting rid of objects, but about reflecting on why we keep them in the first place. Each item represents a memory, a story, or sometimes just a bad purchase made in a gift shop. By documenting these objects before letting them go, I can keep the stories while making space for new experiences in the future.

About the Author

Jessica Savonije

Jessica Savonije is the creator of the Miles of Mess project. She is a student who enjoys traveling, photography, and documenting everyday experiences through small creative projects. Over the years she developed the habit of collecting souvenirs from nearly every place she visited. These objects ranged from traditional travel keepsakes like coins and postcards to completely random items that somehow made their way into her luggage. While each object originally felt meaningful, the growing collection eventually became overwhelming.

The Miles of Mess project grew out of a desire to revisit those objects in a more thoughtful way. Instead of simply throwing things away, Jessica decided to document them first by writing short reflections and photographing the items before removing them from her collection. This process allows her to preserve the stories behind the objects while also reducing the physical clutter they created. The project combines storytelling, photography, and web design into a single ongoing archive.

When she is not working on this project, Jessica enjoys exploring new places, taking photos, and experimenting with creative digital projects. She is particularly interested in how websites can combine visual design with storytelling to create engaging experiences for readers.
